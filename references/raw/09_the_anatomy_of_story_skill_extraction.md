## Runtime extraction

1. Treat story as an organic system: character, plot, theme, world, symbol and scene must influence each other.
2. Begin with a one-sentence premise when building from scratch.
3. Derive a designing principle from the idea instead of imposing a genre formula first.
4. Identify the idea’s inherent promises and craft problems before drafting.
5. Use the seven key steps as the minimal story scaffold: weakness/need, desire, opponent, plan, battle, self-revelation, new equilibrium.
6. For transformation stories, working backward from self-revelation helps define the correct starting need.
7. Keep need and desire separate: inner change requirement versus outer goal.
8. Distinguish psychological need from moral need when the hero’s weakness harms other people.
9. Build the necessary opponent: one capable of attacking the hero’s greatest weakness while competing over the deepest conflict.
10. Build a character web and compare characters by weakness, need, desire, values, power/status/ability, and their response to the central problem.
11. Four-corner opposition is an optional way to maximize value contrast in ensemble stories.
12. Give allies goals of their own when useful.
13. A subplot should compare a related problem and affect the main plot.
14. Express theme primarily through action and consequences, not speeches.
15. Translate structure into story world: spaces, technology, time and environment can embody weakness, opposition and change.
16. Symbol webs should be sparse, relational and developed through repetition-with-variation.
17. The 22 steps are scaffolding, not a fixed formula; shorter stories contract toward the seven core steps.
18. Track revelations as a sequence: logical acquisition, increasing dramatic force, generally increasing density.
19. Build a scene weave / scene list before full drafting for multi-scene stories.
20. For each scene define driver, desire, endpoint, opponent, plan, conflict progression and optional reveal.
21. Enter scenes as late as possible without losing required structure.
22. Use subtext only when the character has a reason not to say the desire directly.
23. Make actions work before dialogue; dialogue should not repair weak structure.
24. Dialogue can carry plot/action, value/moral conflict and recurring key-word/image layers.
25. Every major character should retain a distinct voice.

## AI adaptation notes

- For short manga / anime / video, do not force seven or twenty-two separate shots; structure can be implicit or merged.
- `scene_weave` should feed the Director / Manga planner, not the final prompt directly.
- Story world rules connect directly to Production Design, Architecture and Color/Light modules.
- Symbol and moral-argument systems are optional for lightweight stories.
