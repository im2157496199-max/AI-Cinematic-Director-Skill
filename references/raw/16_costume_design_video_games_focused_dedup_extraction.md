# Costume Design for Video Games — Focused / Deduplicated Skill Extraction

> Source: *Costume Design for Video Games: An Exploration of Historical and Fantastical Skins*, edited by Sandy Appleoff Lyons, CRC Press, 2020.
> Project policy: lightweight costume-design scaffold. Later game/anime artbooks and setting books provide the richer style/case library.
> Runtime focus: Chapters 1–8 general design/pipeline concepts. Historical chapters 9–15 are reference-only unless explicitly requested.

## ACTIVE CORE

1. Costume communicates role, intent, allegiance, experience, status, history, region, and story change; it is not decoration only.
2. Start from costume purpose: activity, role, fantasy/identity, function, milestone, faction, location, occupation, story state.
3. Define constraints before “making it cool”: world rules, body, action needs, medium, output scale, continuity, variant system.
4. Outfit families should share enough design language to belong to one world while allowing controlled variation.
5. Silhouette is the first readability test, especially at small size or distant shots.
6. Validate the actual viewing angle/scale, not only a front beauty pose.
7. Style sheets are handoff interfaces: main view, back/3/4 as needed, component breakdown, accessories, material/fabric callouts, palette/pattern, construction/attachment notes when continuity depends on them, references, motifs.
8. Style-sheet information needs hierarchy; not every callout should be equally dominant.
9. Material changes silhouette and motion through weight, stiffness, thickness, transparency, drape, fold behavior, gloss, layering.
10. Clothing should feel constructed around a body: plausible overlap, attachment, closure, layers, wrap direction unless deliberate abstraction is requested.
11. Accessories can carry story/faction/role cues but need hierarchy; avoid random ornament overload.
12. Cross-cultural inspiration should be deconstructed and reconstructed, not pasted together from recognizable pieces.
13. Research supports construction/context/material logic, then gets transformed through the current project style.
14. Costume must serve story and character consistently.
15. Costume changes are state changes: damage, promotion, season, disguise, ritual, corruption, recovery, faction change, etc.
16. Simplify detail to intended output scale; preserve cues that survive.
17. Use the views production actually needs; orthographic consistency matters, but 3/4 or dominant camera views can be more informative.
18. Keep polycount, ZBrush, morph targets, rigging, cloth simulation and manufacturing specifics reference-only unless explicitly requested.

## REFERENCE_ONLY

## CASE-LIBRARY POLICY
Theory answers: `what must stay coherent?`
Setting books / artbooks answer: `what can look exciting, current, stylish, strange, elegant, youthful, brutal, cute, sacred, grotesque, etc.?`
Case-library style may be stronger than this theory source but must not overwrite identity/continuity locks.
