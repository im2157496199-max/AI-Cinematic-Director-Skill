# Story — Robert McKee — Skill Extraction

> Source role: scene dynamics / value change / conflict / turning points / macro story movement.
> Runtime use: paraphrased rules only. Do not bundle the copyrighted EPUB in public distribution.
> Labels: SOURCE_EXPLICIT / SOURCE_SYNTHESIS / AI_ADAPTATION.

## Core runtime rules

1. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Story Event = meaningful value change.**
   A meaningful event changes a character's life situation in terms of a story value (for example trust/betrayal, freedom/confinement, hope/despair).

2. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Scene must turn.**
   A true scene moves at least one value from one charged state to another with perceptible significance. Activity without value change is not enough.

3. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Beat = exchange of behavior in action/reaction.**
   Beats are the smallest behavioral units inside a scene. Do not confuse them with a pause marker.

4. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Hierarchy of movement.**
   Beats build scenes; scenes build sequences; sequences build acts; acts build toward the story climax.

5. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Sequence has greater impact than its component scenes.**
   A sequence groups scenes so the final scene creates a more determinant change than the previous scenes.

6. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Story Climax produces absolute / irreversible change.**
   Earlier turns can often be reversed; the story climax should establish the decisive final condition.

7. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Five-part macro design.**
   Inciting Incident -> Progressive Complications -> Crisis -> Climax -> Resolution.

8. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Inciting Incident disturbs the balance of the protagonist's life.**
   It launches the central pursuit and must matter enough to demand action.

9. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Progressive Complications escalate through conflict.**
   Do not repeat the same obstacle with cosmetic variation; increase difficulty, stakes, or complexity.

10. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Law of Conflict.**
    Story movement requires conflict. Conflict can come from inner life, relationships, institutions, individuals, or environment.

11. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — The Gap.**
    A character acts expecting one result; reality responds differently or more powerfully. The gap between expectation and result drives further action.

12. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Turning Point changes direction and understanding.**
    A strong scene turn should alter value, force a new choice, reveal information, or reframe what the audience/character understands.

13. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Setup / Payoff must be connected.**
    Plant information or behavior only when it earns later dramatic use; payoff should feel prepared but not obvious.

14. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Crisis is an ultimate dilemma under maximum pressure.**
    It is not merely 'a big problem'; it forces an irreconcilable choice immediately before the decisive action.

15. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Climax answers the central dramatic question through action.**
    The climax should be the final, most concentrated expression of the story's core conflict.

16. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Resolution shows the resulting condition.**
    Use only as much resolution as needed to let the audience absorb the final value state and remaining consequences.

17. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Principle of Antagonism.**
    A protagonist becomes as compelling as the total forces opposing the protagonist's will and desire.

18. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Antagonism is broader than a villain.**
    Include inner contradiction, personal conflict, institutions, society, time, physical limits, and environment when relevant.

19. **SOURCE_SYNTHESIS — Escalate negative pressure qualitatively, not only quantitatively.**
    Strong escalation changes the kind of problem, not merely the amount of the same problem.

20. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Characterization is not True Character.**
    Observable traits, appearance, mannerisms, speech, occupation, lifestyle and attitudes describe characterization; true character is revealed by consequential choices.

21. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — True Character appears through choice under pressure.**
    The greater and more meaningful the pressure, the more revealing the choice.

22. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Character dimension comes from consistent contradiction.**
    Random quirks do not create depth. Contradictions must arise from a coherent character.

23. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Text and subtext are separate layers.**
    Text is visible/audible behavior and speech; subtext is the desire, thought, feeling, or impulse beneath the surface.

24. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Fix scene action before polishing dialogue.**
    Rephrasing dialogue cannot rescue a scene whose behavioral exchanges and turns are structurally weak.

25. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Exposition is secondary to immediate drama.**
    Dramatize necessary facts inside conflict/action. Do not create scenes whose only purpose is to explain background.

26. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Composition requires unity and variety.**
    The story should feel causally unified while scene type, location, rhythm, value, and emotional texture vary.

27. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Dialogue is not ordinary conversation.**
    Screen dialogue is compressed, directional, purposeful, and built around scene turns while still sounding speakable.

## Integration decisions

- Keep Truby's `premise / designing principle / seven-step core / character web / scene weave`.
- Use McKee primarily to strengthen **scene-level dynamics**, **value turns**, **gap / expectation-result logic**, **macro escalation**, and **choice under pressure**.
- Do not force McKee's feature-film scale onto short manga/anime/video.
- A manga panel is not automatically a Beat, and a Beat is not automatically a camera shot.
- For short-form work, multiple beats can live inside one panel/shot; one decisive beat can also justify a cut when visually useful.
