# 荒木飞吕彦的漫画术 — Skill Extraction

> Source role: Japanese manga creation / sequential storytelling / NAME / panel rhythm / character readability.
> Source: user-supplied Chinese fan-translation PDF.
> Public distribution rule: do NOT bundle the source PDF. Store only paraphrased, original extraction notes.
> Labels: SOURCE_EXPLICIT / SOURCE_SYNTHESIS / AI_ADAPTATION.

## A. Medium model

1. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Manga is a combined system, not isolated drawing.**
   Diagnose the work through four interacting dimensions:
   - character
   - story
   - story world
   - theme
   Drawing integrates them; dialogue supports them.

2. **SOURCE_SYNTHESIS — Do not force equal weight.**
   A work may emphasize one dimension, but the other dimensions should support rather than contradict it.

3. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Treat the method as a map, not a fixed formula.**
   The source explicitly warns against copying the method mechanically. Runtime rules therefore remain conditional and style-agnostic.

## B. Opening / reader pull

4. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Page 1 must create a reason to continue.**
   The opening should communicate enough identity, novelty, atmosphere, mystery, character, or dramatic promise that the reader wants the next page.

5. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — First-panel 5W1H is a baseline, not a mandatory layout.**
   Time / place / person / action can establish orientation, but the creator may deliberately bend the baseline to create mystery or individuality.

6. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — One panel can carry multiple layers of information.**
   Prefer images/actions/dialogue that simultaneously communicate character, setting, situation, relationship, and tone instead of separate exposition panels.

7. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — The opening functions as a preview of the work.**
   It should signal what kind of experience the reader is entering without explaining everything.

8. **AI_ADAPTATION — Opening hook check.**
   For short AI manga, the first 1–3 panels should answer:
   - What is immediately interesting?
   - What question or tension is opened?
   - What visual/character promise does the sequence make?
   - Why should the viewer continue?

## C. Character readability

9. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — A strong character can be recognized in a few lines of description.**
   The character should have a clear core identity rather than a pile of interchangeable adjectives.

10. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — The author needs more character knowledge than the page displays.**
    Background information may remain unseen, but it should help answer: “What would this character do here?”

11. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Character dossiers can prevent homogenization.**
    The source uses a large profile sheet; the runtime Skill should not require every field, but should keep enough personal/history/taste/fear/value data to produce stable choices.

12. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Include weaknesses and effort/growth, not only strengths.**
    A flaw becomes dramatically useful when it creates difficulty and the character struggles with or grows around it.

13. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Differentiate the cast by contrast.**
    Supporting characters should not be clones of the protagonist; contrast can operate through values, temperament, behavior, visual identity, role, rhythm, and relationship.

14. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Research behavior specific to role/status/culture.**
    Small accurate actions, manners, vocabulary, occupational habits, and etiquette make character performance more convincing.

15. **SOURCE_SYNTHESIS — Character and story should advance together.**
    Prefer scenes/panels that both move the situation and reveal how the character behaves.

## D. Story movement for manga

16. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Avoid “zero-sum” progression.**
    Do not create a long detour that merely returns the protagonist to the exact original dramatic state unless the return itself produces meaningful new value, knowledge, or character change.

17. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Coincidence should not solve the protagonist's central problem without setup.**
    Resolutions should emerge from character, story logic, world rules, or established setup.

18. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Do not create trouble by making a competent character inexplicably stupid.**
    Difficulty should arise from meaningful limitation, pressure, misinformation, values, or opposition.

19. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Protect reader investment.**
    Twists/endings should not casually invalidate the reader's accumulated engagement.

20. **AI_ADAPTATION — Forward-motion test.**
    Each short sequence should produce at least one net gain/loss/change in:
    - knowledge
    - relationship
    - danger
    - freedom
    - capability
    - commitment
    - location / access
    - emotional stance

## E. Story world / theme

21. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Story world includes everyday and extraordinary space.**
    Architecture, objects, customs, environment, technology, manners, and background behavior all participate.

22. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Research can turn world detail into dramatic pressure.**
    Do not research only for decoration; use the researched rule, object, ritual, job, or etiquette to create decisions and consequences.

23. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Theme connects character, story, and world.**
    Theme works best as an organizing force rather than a direct lecture.

24. **SOURCE_SYNTHESIS — Worldbuilding should remain proportional to the task.**
    Short manga/video does not need an encyclopedia. Keep only world rules that affect visible action, choices, atmosphere, or continuity.

## F. Drawing / visual identity

25. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Manga art must be readable and distinctive, not merely technically beautiful.**
    Visual identity matters because the reader should recognize the work quickly.

26. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Combine realism and symbolization selectively.**
    Render important information with enough specificity while simplifying less important information so the eye knows where to look.

27. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Avoid equal-detail overload.**
    If everything is rendered with the same density and emphasis, visual priority becomes unclear.

28. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — A single image/panel can have stand-alone impact.**
    Composition, color, expression, line/shape, and moment selection can compress a large amount of meaning into one frozen image.

29. **SOURCE_SYNTHESIS — Style is not fixed by the book.**
    JOJO-style visual language may be selected explicitly as a valid project style/reference, but it must not be auto-applied merely because Araki is a source. Concrete art style comes from user intent / project references / style library.

## G. Script -> page budget -> paneling -> NAME

30. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Work backward from fixed page/space budget.**
    Allocate more page/panel space to more important moments instead of distributing space evenly.

31. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Determine what you most want the reader to receive.**
    Page allocation and panel size should reflect dramatic/emotional importance.

32. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Panel size is part of rhythm.**
    Larger/smaller panels create emphasis and pacing; page rhythm and whole-sequence rhythm both matter.

33. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Repeating the same panel pattern for too long causes fatigue.**
    Vary scale, framing, density, whitespace, and panel geometry when the content or emotional rhythm asks for it.

34. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Panel design may expose emotion that the main spatial view hides.**
    Example principle: if the body orientation hides the face, an inserted close-up can restore emotional readability.

35. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — NAME is a planning layer, not final rendering.**
    Use rough sequential layouts to test action, psychology, expression, page rhythm, and transitions before expensive final generation.

36. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Japanese manga NAME often gives strong attention to internal emotion and facial reaction.**
    Do not reduce a manga adaptation to a list of physical actions.

37. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Preserve spatial orientation across a local action passage.**
    Avoid random camera-side changes that confuse where characters are.

38. **AI_ADAPTATION — Panel importance score.**
    Rank candidate panels:
    - P0: indispensable turn / hook / reveal / emotional peak
    - P1: necessary action / relation / orientation
    - P2: useful transition
    - P3: decorative / redundant

39. **AI_ADAPTATION — Panel-to-video mapping is selective.**
    Do not animate every panel. Promote P0/P1 panels or important transitions into video shots; use P2 panels as bridges or omit them.

## H. Dialogue

40. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Dialogue should sound natural for the character, not “writerly” for its own sake.**
    Vocabulary and tone should arise from role, background, attitude, and current relationship.

41. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Dialogue can communicate story, character, and world at the same time.**
    Prefer lines that do more than one job.

42. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Research what the character would and would not say.**
    Occupational/cultural vocabulary and forbidden/unlikely phrasing are useful voice constraints.

43. **SOURCE_SYNTHESIS — Dialogue remains subordinate to behavior and image.**
    If the same information can be shown clearly through action, expression, object, environment, or panel relation, do not duplicate it in speech.

## I. Idea workflow

44. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — A practical manga workflow can move through:**
    idea notes -> development notes -> script -> NAME -> final art.

45. **SOURCE_EXPLICIT — Short works benefit from one strong core idea.**
    Multiple unrelated “cool ideas” can make a short piece muddy.

46. **AI_ADAPTATION — AI workflow translation.**
    User idea
    -> story/character spec
    -> manga core premise
    -> page/sequence budget
    -> script beats
    -> NAME-like rough panel plan
    -> panel specs
    -> image generation
    -> continuity repair
    -> select key panels for video
    -> video shots / edit.
