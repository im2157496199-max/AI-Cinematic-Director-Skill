## Dedup result

Most of Barnwell's broad production-design principles overlap with LoBrutto and therefore are **not added again** as separate runtime rules. The useful delta is concentrated in place-as-agent, spatial familiarity, design visibility, visual polarisation, story-order progression, and temporal identity.

## NEW_ACTIVE — rules that materially extend

### B01 — Place is an active narrative variable
The same action/dialogue can mean something different in a different place. Treat location selection as part of story design, not merely a container for action.

Runtime implication:
- define why *this* place is the right place for the scene;
- when useful, let the environment actively help, constrain, tempt, expose, isolate, or oppose the character.

### B02 — Environment can function as character or antagonist
A set/location may have narrative agency rather than staying passive background. This is stronger than the existing `psychological_effect` field: the environment may create obstacles and alter what actions are possible.

### B03 — Spatial familiarity can become dramatic syntax
When a recurring location is used repeatedly, the audience learns its geography. That learned pattern creates rhythm and expectation. A deliberate break in access, ownership, route, room-use, or vertical status can therefore carry story meaning.

### B04 — Curate the real world; do not merely reproduce it
Observed/real locations contain conflicting visual information. Select, remove, disguise, or emphasize details so the intended meaning is legible.

Anti-cliché condition:
- do not reduce a city/culture/period to the most picturesque, exotic, tourist-like, or stereotyped shorthand;
- identify the **dominant characteristic relevant to this story**, not the most familiar icon.

### B05 — Choose a design-visibility mode deliberately
Effective production design is not always invisible. A project may intentionally choose:
- `supportive_invisible`
- `expressive_visible`
- `spectacle_forward`

Spectacle is not automatically a failure. It fails when its prominence conflicts with the narrative purpose rather than being an intentional part of that purpose.

### B06 — Visual polarisation can externalize the story's key contrast
A central opposition in the drama can be expressed across environments through controlled contrast in shape, texture, volume, density, material, palette, order, or scale.

Do not turn every project into a binary design. Use only when the story genuinely contains a useful polarity.

### B07 — Evaluate production design in story order, not only location-by-location
Place reference images / location designs in narrative sequence and inspect the visual progression as a whole.

Track:
- escalation / release
- increasing or decreasing density
- palette changes
- spatial compression / expansion
- repeated images or materials
- when a familiar space changes meaning

### B08 — Motif recurrence should have a sequence map
A key color, material, object, shape, architectural detail, or environmental condition may recur across the story to build recognition, atmosphere, or inevitability.

Record where it appears and what changes in its meaning. Do not repeat a motif merely for branding.

### B09 — Design negative space and omission, not only objects
Gaps, bare walls, darkness, missing detail, empty volume, and unfilled areas can be active design choices. A place can become more subjective or dreamlike by withholding expected environmental information.

This rule stays at the Production Design level: cinematographic composition remains owned by the existing visual/camera layer.

### B10 — Temporal identity is more than surface accuracy
For past/present/future settings, distinguish:
- factual accuracy
- period spirit / social logic
- selective shorthand
- stylised reinterpretation
- the unavoidable influence of the era in which the work is made

A museum-like copy can be visually accurate yet dramatically empty. Conversely, an invented future should still support the narrative rather than merely look “futuristic.”

### B11 — Contemporary design also requires authorship
Present-day settings are not neutral. Fashionable objects, materials, architecture, UI, branding, and technology can date a work quickly. Decide which contemporary signals are essential and which are accidental trend noise.

### B12 — Source strategy is a creative decision
For each key environment choose one:
- `observed_real`
- `transformed_real`
- `constructed`
- `hybrid`
- `virtual`

“Real” does not automatically mean more truthful, and “constructed” does not automatically mean less authentic. The criterion is narrative, atmospheric, spatial, and production usefulness.

## MERGE_EXISTING — supported by Barnwell but already active

These ideas are **not duplicated** in the runtime core:

- story over personal design ambition -> already `Story alignment / style not imposed`
- concept before details -> already `Design concept first`
- research/reference -> already `Research -> transform`
- character expressed through possessions and environment -> already `Character-space relation / décor / signs of use`
- color as story/character/mood -> already `Color`
- texture/material as meaning and authenticity -> already `Material / texture / age`
- architecture/space as emotional/narrative language -> already `Architecture / space`
- props and set dressing -> already `Props and décor`
- previsualization/sketch/storyboard/model -> already `Previsualization / approval gate`
- collaboration with cinematography / motivated light -> already `lighting_interaction` and camera-compatible environment
- CGI/digital design should serve story -> already `Digital / AI rule`
- stylised/nonrealistic design can be valid -> already `Style flexibility`

## REFERENCE_ONLY — useful context, not runtime load

- history of production designer / art director titles
- studio-system department structure
- budget and schedule negotiations
- construction staffing / procurement workflow
- technical drawing history
- historical film-stock / screen-format technology details
- obsolete or medium-specific special-effects procedures
- awards history and institutional recognition

## AI adaptation summary

Barnwell's unique contribution to this Skill is therefore **not another general Production Design rulebook**. It becomes a delta layer that asks:

```text
WHY THIS PLACE?
    ↓
WHAT DOES THE PLACE DO TO THE CHARACTER?
    ↓
WHAT VISUAL SIGNALS SHOULD BE REMOVED / EMPHASIZED?
    ↓
HOW VISIBLE SHOULD THE DESIGN ITSELF BE?
    ↓
WHAT CENTRAL CONTRAST ORGANIZES THE WORLD?
    ↓
HOW DOES THE DESIGN CHANGE IN STORY ORDER?
    ↓
WHAT RECURS, AND HOW DOES ITS MEANING CHANGE?
    ↓
WHAT TIME-IDENTITY IS INTENTIONAL VS ACCIDENTAL?
```
