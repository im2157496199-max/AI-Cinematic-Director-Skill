## SOURCE ROLE

This book is valuable for showing design with images rather than abstract theory.
The strongest transferable material is:
- color-wheel based palette balancing
- skin-base variation
- hair/eye/clothing color coordination
- multi-character palette contrast
- brightness/saturation control for "cute" readability.

## ACTIVE RULES

### CB01 — Palette design begins with hierarchy, not with many pretty colors
Choose:
- dominant color family
- support color family
- accent color
- neutral / grounding areas
Do not give equal visual weight to every hue.

### CB02 — Skin base affects all later choices
The book shows yellow-base, pink-base, and pale/white-base skin as meaningful starting families.
Portable runtime rule:
- establish skin temperature first
- then harmonize hair, eye, and outfit decisions against it.

### CB03 — Hair is usually the main color mass; eyes are often the accent focus
A useful default for anime/moe design:
- hair = large color mass / identity field
- eyes = small but high-attention accent
- costume = support or world/faction carrier
This is a common structure, not a law.

### CB04 — Complementary and near-complementary accents tighten the design
The sample pages show that accent colors close to a complementary relation often improve focus.
For runtime:
- use high-contrast accents sparingly
- keep the face area as the main beneficiary of accent contrast.

### CB05 — Similar hues create cohesion; contrast hues create cast separation
For ensemble design:
- use related hues when you want one team/faction feel
- use contrast on one or two axes when you need fast individual readability.
Do not separate characters by hue alone; combine with silhouette and behavior.

### CB06 — Brightness and saturation are emotional controls
The examples show that keeping value mid/high while raising saturation creates a more energetic, vivid, "cute" finish.
Portable rule:
- saturation increases excitement / youth / immediacy
- lower saturation or darker value can imply restraint, heaviness, maturity, or coolness.
Use this relationally rather than as a stereotype table.

### CB07 — Face-area color contrast has priority
The audience reads the face first.
Reserve your strongest local contrast for:
- hair framing the face
- eye color vs surrounding skin/hair
- accessory accents near the head
Do not place all strongest accents on shoes or hem details unless that is deliberate.

### CB08 — Outfit palette should support role and personality, not compete with the face
Costume colors can communicate:
- school/faction identity
- neatness vs flamboyance
- maturity vs youth
- warmth vs distance
Avoid outfit schemes that overpower the character's facial read unless spectacle is the point.

### CB09 — Repeated color motifs can unify a design
A small eye-color echo in ribbon, trim, tie, device-light, or lining can help cohesion.
Use echoes lightly; over-repetition becomes mechanical.

### CB10 — Group palettes need controlled contrast axes
When several characters share a setting/uniform:
- vary one or two major colors
- vary brightness or saturation
- keep some common neutral or institutional anchor
This preserves group belonging without collapse into sameness.

### CB11 — Mini/chibi readability benefits from simpler color blocks
In smaller or simplified characters:
- reduce palette complexity
- increase silhouette clarity
- keep the face and one identity accent readable at small scale.
This merges with the mini-character source already integrated in the character-design layer.

### CB12 — Printed recipes are reference-only; relational logic is runtime-active
Exact CMYK/page formulas are not portable across digital pipelines.
What transfers is the relation among skin, hair, eyes, and costume.

## REFERENCE_ONLY

- exact print values
- one-era moe palette trends as universal default
- using saturated color alone to force cuteness

## STYLE FIREWALL

DO NOT:
- differentiate characters only by hair color
- ignore skin temperature when selecting hair/eye colors
- overfill a design with equally loud rainbow accents
- let outfit accents steal the face focal priority by accident
