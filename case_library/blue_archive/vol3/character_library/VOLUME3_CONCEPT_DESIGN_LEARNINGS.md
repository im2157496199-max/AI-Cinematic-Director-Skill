# Volume 3 concept-design learnings

Reviewed p.132–185.

## What these sheets teach
1. Character identity is a system, not a single outfit: full body, alternate views, accessories, weapon, halo, SD/chibi and small prop drawings are shown together.
2. Shared faction language is established through repeated color, trim, emblem and material cues, while each student keeps one or two stronger individual anchors.
3. Props are not decoration. Bags, food, tools, weapons, mascots and devices explain role, hobby or routine.
4. Alternate costume or event designs preserve face/hair/halo/weapon identity while changing silhouette and seasonal material.
5. The SD/chibi version is an excellent stress test: if the identity collapses when simplified, the design is too dependent on minor details.
6. Mechanical designs are documented through orthographic views and component breakdowns; AI generation should similarly separate identity-critical components from decorative ones.
7. Color blocks are cleaner than they appear at first glance: the number of major color masses is limited, with small saturated accents used for recognition.

## AI transfer
For derivative work, create a lock sheet before prompting:
`face + hair silhouette + halo + school/faction motif + weapon/prop + dominant palette`.
Then vary pose, action, lighting and environment.
